## 0. The ten things that matter most (short version)

1. **Never lose a good moment.** The strongest modern trend is *retrospective* capture: the instrument is always recording, and you decide afterwards what becomes a loop (Ableton Capture MIDI, Loopy Pro retrospective record, Chase Bliss Mood/Habit, Red Panda Tensor, Mutable Beads/Clouds). It also removes the hardest beginner problem: pressing record exactly on the downbeat.
2. **The seam must be inaudible.** Solid loopers crossfade at the loop point and at every punch-in/out (SooperLooper, softcut, oooooo), and the good ones record *margins* beyond the loop so the crossfade has real material (Deluge) or fold the tail of the last note back into the start (Loopy Pro "tail").
3. **Time is negotiated, not dictated.** Good designs let you start anywhere and still round the length to the grid (SooperLooper "rel sync", Loopy Pro "phase preservation"), let the first free loop set the tempo (Ableton "Set & Follow", Octatrack pickup master), and keep everything locked afterwards (play sync, phase lock).
4. **Layers are the instrument.** Undo/redo depth, per-layer access ("peel"), decay of old layers (Frippertronics, Blooper Repeats, Ditto Decay), and replace/substitute/insert/multiply (the EDP vocabulary) are what make a looper playable over a whole set rather than for one build-up.
5. **Transformation is where artistry happens.** Varispeed with tape inertia, stepped speed in musical intervals, reverse at the next boundary, half speed, trimming loops within loops, scrambling slices, dropping pieces, envelope-ducking, granular reading, wear/degradation – and the option to *imprint* a transformation into the loop by overdubbing through it (Blooper "Additive", SooperLooper rate change during record).
6. **Several loops of different lengths** – synchronised multiples (polymeter) or deliberately asynchronous (Eno, Reich, Hainbach) – is the single most powerful structural idea in the history of looping.
7. **Form needs scenes.** Song forms come from muting/unmuting, series loops (verse/chorus of different lengths), scenes, follow actions and quantised switching (RC-505, Ditto/Infinity series mode, Octatrack scenes and crossfader, Ableton/Bitwig/Loopy follow/next actions).
8. **Symbolic loops and audio loops are complementary, not rivals.** Event loops stay alive (re-voiceable, transposable, editable, re-playable on another sound); audio loops capture the exact performance including space and effects and can be mangled like tape. The best modern systems (Ableton, Deluge, Loopy Pro 2, Octatrack) let you go from one to the other.
9. **State must be visible.** Circular progress per loop (Loopy), pending-action indicators, what is live vs what is loop. Hidden modes are the main source of "unreliable" feelings.
10. **One hand (or no hands).** Single-button record→play/overdub cycles, long-press = momentary, double-tap = undo, threshold-armed recording, count-in and auto count-out, foot or MIDI control – on a phone also Bluetooth page-turner pedals and gyro.

## 1. Devices and software

### 1.1 Multi-track phrase loopers ("loop stations")

**Boss RC-505mkII / RC-600.** Five (505mkII, tabletop) or six (600, floor) stereo tracks; tracks in parallel or series; per-track settings for playback, level, start/stop mode, dub mode, FX, loop sync, tempo sync, bounce-in and input ([RC-505mkII manual via manuals.plus](https://manuals.plus/m/19f267c27cfbf6b44e1ee37a8cca3ffa695fb5dfa6c6ce7a75cfff524974626f); [RC-600 Parameter Guide](https://static.roland.com/assets/media/pdf/RC-600_Parameter_eng01_W.pdf)). What matters musically:
- **REC ACTION** decides the one-button cycle (record → overdub or record → play), plus retrigger and one-shot playback; **AUTO REC** starts recording when you start playing; **START MODE** immediate or fade-in; **STOP MODE** (immediate / fade-out / at loop end, the last unverified); **DUB MODE** overdub or replace ([RC-600 Parameter Guide](https://static.roland.com/assets/media/pdf/RC-600_Parameter_eng01_W.pdf)).
- **LOOP SYNC** makes later tracks multiples of the first, and track lengths must be multiples of one another – that is what allows polymeter, but Sound on Sound found polymetric recording "could be better handled" and missed a bar counter ([SOS review](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/boss-rc-505-mkii)).
- **Bounce** consolidates several tracks into one to free tracks; **Mark Back** jumps back to a marked loop state ([Rekkerd](https://rekkerd.org/mini-review-boss-rc-505-mkii-loop-station/); [Boss](https://in.boss.info/products/rc-505mk2/)).
- 49 input FX and 53 track FX (including beat-synced stutter, filter, slicer); global fade-in/out times on track start/stop; tempo changes of about ±20 BPM with little degradation ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/reviews/boss-rc-505-mkii)). 99 memories with six tracks each ([Reverb](https://reverb.com/item/53129602-boss-rc-600-loop-station-rc-600)).
- Lesson: very reliable and fast, but "phrase recorder" thinking – structure lives in which tracks are muted, transformation is limited to FX.

**Headrush Looperboard.** Four tracks per loop with five track modes (Fixed, Serial, Sync, Serial/Sync, Free); a master track defines the length, others must match or be multiples; auto BPM detection when recording; tap tempo with time-stretch that keeps pitch; touchscreen ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/get-eight-hours-of-recording-time-with-headrushs-new-looperboard/); [Headrush FAQ](https://support.headrushfx.com/en/support/solutions/articles/69000801163-headrush-looperboard-frequently-asked-questions)). Lesson: making the length relation of each track an explicit *mode* (fixed/serial/sync/free) is clearer than one global sync switch.

**TC Electronic Ditto X4.** Two loops, **Sync** (both play, second a multiple of the first) or **Serial** (one after the other – verse/chorus); a **Decay** knob sets how much each loop loses per repeat; Loop FX: reverse, half/double speed (octave), tape stop, fade, stutter/hold; MIDI sync ([Mixdown](https://mixdownmag.com.au/?p=34544); [Guitar.com](https://guitar.com/review/tc-electronic-ditto-x4-looper-review/); [ModularGrid](https://modulargrid.net/p/offers/view/1318645)). Lesson: "Decay" and "tape stop" as first-class buttons are what players love most.

**Pigtronix Infinity.** Two loops, series or parallel; **Sync Multi** sets loop 2 to 1, 2, 3, 4 or 6× loop 1 – note the **3× and 6×**, i.e. odd ratios for polymeter; series mode switches between sections of different length (2-bar verse, 4-bar chorus) in sync; MIDI clock sync so start/stop points obey the DAW; split input so each loop has its own source ([Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/gear/pigtronix-infinity-looper-pedal-review); [Guitar.com](https://guitar.com/review/pigtronix-infinity-looper-review/)).

**Electro-Harmonix 95000.** Six mono tracks plus a stereo mixdown track per loop (a multitrack *recorder* used as a looper); quantised or free-running record; speed over 2 octaves; reverse playback **and reverse recording**; overdub or punch in/out; tap BPM; MIDI clock ([effectsdatabase](https://www.effectsdatabase.com/model/eh/95000); [Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/electro-harmonix-unveils-the-95000-performance-loop-laboratory)). Reggie Watts used its predecessors 2880/45000 ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/the-gadgets-of-music-genius-reggie-watts-5913895)).

**Line 6 DL4 MkII.** The classic DL4 looper (Andrew Bird, Reggie Watts) is preserved: Record/Overdub, Play/Stop, Play Once, **Half Speed**, **Reverse** on footswitches; 240 s mono/120 s stereo, hours with microSD; half-time and reverse can be overdubbed back into the loop ([SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/line-6-dl4-mkii); [Perfect Circuit](https://www.perfectcircuit.com/line-6-dl4-mkii.html)). The original DL4 had about 14 s (28 s at half speed) *(unverified)* – the half-speed trick to "buy" loop time, with its darker, grainier sound, became a style.

**Eventide H90 looper.** Separate **record speed and play speed** (since 1.9.10): record at one speed, play back at another, arbitrary or quantised resolution ("Smooth" vs stepped), reverse and full/half speed as performance parameters; forum reports of drift with tempo sync on older firmware ([Eventide release notes](https://eventideaudio.com/?p=187149); [forum](https://www.eventideaudio.com/forums/topic/h90-looper-playback-speed-varies-jumps/)). Lesson: rec-speed ≠ play-speed is the tape trick (record at half speed, play at normal = octave up and twice as dense).

**Empress Zoia.** A modular grid where "Looper" is a module: length, start position, speed and reverse are all *modulatable* by envelopes, LFOs, expression pedal – users drive loop length from an envelope follower on the input ([Elektronauts](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/empress-zoia-euroburo/50119?page=17); [Patchstorage](https://patchstorage.com/glitchy-micro-sampler-w-reverse-option/)). Lesson: loop parameters as modulation destinations (Myzel's mod layer).

### 1.2 The Echoplex lineage and software loopers

**Echoplex Digital Pro (Paradis LOOP 1992 → Gibson EDP 1994 → LoopIV).** Designed by Matthias Grob (with Keith McMillen, Kim Flint): one memory treated like a tape echo, record and playback positions cycling, loops not fixed like samples – length and start point can change at any moment ([Looper's Delight](https://loopersdelight.com/tools/echoplex/echoplex.html); [history](https://loopersdelight.com/tools/echoplex/OBechoplexhistory.html); [Evoloop](https://www.evoloop.matthiasgrob.org/)). The EDP vocabulary (Multiply, Insert, Replace, Substitute, Mute, NextLoop, SyncRecord, Quantize, RoundMode) is still the most complete theory of *live* loop editing; SooperLooper and Mobius copy it.

**SooperLooper (open source, read in full).** Modelled "in the spirit of the EDP LoopIII and LoopIV" ([features](https://github.com/essej/sooperlooper/blob/master/doc/html/features.html)). The essentials, from its [command reference](https://github.com/essej/sooperlooper/blob/master/doc/html/doc_commands.html) and [sync page](https://github.com/essej/sooperlooper/blob/master/doc/html/doc_sync.html):
- **Record** manual or by input threshold; *"stop and start recording the moment you hit the downbeat, not before. Trust them."* **Special endings:** ending a record with Overdub/Multiply/Insert/Replace/Substitute/Once finishes the loop and starts that function at once.
- **Overdub** with **Feedback** (amount of old loop kept per pass – "when Feedback is set to 100%, it uses a slightly less amount to help prevent clipping").
- **Multiply** repeats the loop under you, making a 1-bar loop 2, 3 … bars ("cycles"); **MultiIncrease** lets you pre-count extra cycles. **Insert** adds a cycle of new material in place. **Replace** records over without hearing the old; **Substitute** replaces but *you still hear the old material while playing* – "helps maintain the groove".
- **Reverse** any time, even during overdub, or quantised to the next boundary; **Trigger**/**Once** (play once, then mute); **Mute**/**Pause**; **Solo** across loops.
- **Undo/Redo** "nearly unlimited", reclaiming oldest states when memory runs out; Undo also *cancels a pending quantised operation*; the verse/chorus trick (record a verse, re-record a chorus, Undo/Redo switches between them).
- **Rate shift** 1/4× to 4×, *also while recording*, affecting incoming audio "reminiscent of tape delay systems"; **Scratch**; **Delay mode** (tap two presses = delay time).
- **Long-press (>1 s) turns any command into a momentary (SUS) one**: release ends it.
- **Sync** to internal tempo, MIDI clock, JACK or another loop; quantize Off/Cycle/8th/Loop; **rel sync**: "start recording a new loop at any time and it will keep track of the sync and round off the loop so the length is a multiple of the sync interval"; **play sync** retriggers the loop near boundaries to stay locked to drifting MIDI clock (no stretching).
- **Crossfades** "applied to prevent clicks on loop or edit operation boundaries (crossfade length is adjustable)".
- **Latency compensation** with separate input and output latency (quoted in §3.1).

**Mobius (Circular Labs).** Eight synchronised stereo loopers with copying of loops and of *timing* between them, scripting ([KVR](https://www.kvraudio.com/product/mobius-by-circular-labs)). Ed Sheeran's "Chewie Monsta" is a foot controller driving Mobius in Ableton: four loop tracks (GUITAR, BOOM = body percussion, VOX …) that the front-of-house engineer mixes and effects *separately*; the Mobius display is mirrored onto screens in the pedalboard so he can see record/overdub/play state ([Reverb](https://reverb.com/news/ed-sheerans-chewie-looper-crashes-equipboard-gear-site); [Equipboard](https://equipboard.com/items/chewie-monsta-looper)).

**Expert Sleepers Augustus Loop.** A tape delay that is also a looper: virtual tape with speed changes, reverse, stop with inertia ("tape-like behaviour when changing delay times"), Tape Sim degradation, filter and saturation in the feedback path, tap-to-set loop length while recording the first phrase, host-tempo sync, punch in/out following the host, automatic fades, delays up to 3600 s ([Expert Sleepers](https://www.expert-sleepers.co.uk/augustusloop.html); [Rekkerd](https://rekkerd.org/expert-sleepers-updates-augustus-loop-to-v21)). Lesson: "delay with infinite feedback" and "looper" are the same machine; inertia makes speed changes musical.

**Ableton Live: Looper, Session clips, Capture MIDI.** Looper: one multi-purpose transport button whose function "changes in a predetermined sequence so that you can control Looper from a single MIDI button or pedal"; when Live plays, Looper acts like a clip and follows launch quantisation; when Live is stopped, it starts immediately; **Tempo Control** None / Follow song tempo / *Set & Follow* – with the set stopped, Looper analyses the first recording and sets Live's tempo ([Live manual, audio effects](https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/live-audio-effect-reference/); [Loopmasters](https://www.loopmasters.com/articles/2187-Ableton-Live-Devices-How-To-Use-Live-Looper)). Further controls *(unverified this session, from the manual)*: Record length in bars or open, ×2 (double by repeating) and ÷2, Reverse, Speed (with octave buttons), Feedback, Input→Output monitoring modes (forum: decides whether you hear yourself, yourself + loop, or loop only – [Ableton forum](https://forum.ableton.com/viewtopic.php?p=889303)), "Drag me" to turn the loop into a clip. **Capture MIDI**: Live constantly listens on armed/monitored MIDI tracks; pressing Capture makes a clip from what you just played; if the transport is stopped and the set is empty, it detects the tempo and loop from the played material and starts playback ([Ableton help](https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000776450-Capture-MIDI)). Session view: clips in scenes, launch quantisation, follow actions for generative/song structures.

**Bitwig Studio.** Clip launcher with **Next Actions** (Continue, Stop, Return, Next + 20 targets), project defaults overridable per slot; since 5.0 launch modes that continue relative to the playing clip or the timeline, and **actions on release** – hold a pad to play a clip, release to return ([MusicTech review](https://musictech.com/reviews/digital-audio-workstations/bitwig-studio-5-continues-to-innovate-with-a-new-tranche-of-creative-tools); [Bitwig user guide](https://www.bitwig.com/userguide/latest/triggering_launcher_clips_0)). Lesson: momentary clip launching is ideal for touch.

**Loopy Pro (iOS; A Tasty Pixel).** The richest phone/tablet looper; a configurable canvas of clips, faders, buttons. From the [Loop Academy](https://loopypro.com/tutorials/loop-academy/), [manual](https://loopypro.com/manual/) and [wiki](https://wiki.loopypro.com/Introduction_to_Loopy_Pro):
- Recording settings: **retrospective recording** (quantised = last complete cycle; immediate = a cycle-length ending now, rotated to stay phase-locked), **auto count-in and count-out**, **threshold recording**, **intro and tail** (material before the loop start / after its end, blended into the loop), **overdub after recording**, **phase preservation**, wait-for-playback, **auto loop detection**.
- Playback settings: play/stop quantisation, **phase lock** (lost when you change a clip's speed), time stretch, one-shot quantisation, fades, record-when-empty.
- **Follow actions** and an actions system bound to gestures or MIDI; Ableton Link.
- 2.0 (2025): **MIDI clips** with piano-roll, groove quantise (swing, randomness, custom groove), overdub with "peel/replace" ([Synth Anatomy](https://synthanatomy.com/2025/07/loopy-pro-2-0-levels-up-the-creative-audio-looper-app-with-midi-looping-and-more.html); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/loopy-pro-looper-daw-sampler/id1492670451)).
- Users report free-length tempo detection works with clear transients but jumps to double tempo on slow material (55 → 110 BPM); setting the bar count is "more foolproof" ([Loopy forum](https://forum.loopypro.com/discussion/comment/749039/)).

### 1.3 Experimental pedals: the looper as a sound-shaping instrument

**Chase Bliss Blooper ("bottomless looper", with Knobs).** The reference for *creative* looping ([product](https://www.chasebliss.com/blooper); [modifiers](https://blooper.chasebliss.com/modifiers); [manual](https://blooper.chasebliss.com/resources/docs/manual.pdf); [cheat sheet](https://blooper.chasebliss.com/resources/docs/modifier-manual.pdf)):
- **Three modes.** *Normal*: modifiers act after the loop, the loop stays clean. *Additive*: overdubbing while a modifier or Stability is active **imprints** it into the loop. *Sampler*: each new recording replaces the old (and loops by default).
- **Modifiers** (two channels A/B with three each; every knob has a neutral 12 o'clock and different behaviours on either side): *Smooth Speed* (up to 2×, down to stop, CCW = reverse); *Stepped Speed* (jumps in octaves and fifths "tuned to stay in key", up to +2 oct); *Trimmer* (CCW moves the end towards the start, CW the start towards the end – loops within loops; stepped/speed variants snap to musical subdivisions); *Dropper* (pieces drop out, random CCW or a repeating pattern CW); *Scrambler* (re-orders the loop, random or repeating sequence); *Swapper* (envelope-sensitive muting: the loop goes silent when you play – in Additive mode this replaces parts of the loop with your playing); *Stopper* (CW volume fade, CCW "melting" tape stop); *Filter*; *Pitcher* (±3 oct, speed unchanged); *Stretcher* (time-stretch, pitch unchanged); *Stutter* (CCW repeats what just happened, CW what comes next) ([search summaries of the above](https://www.chasebliss.com/blooper)).
- **Stability**: wow, flutter, noise, filtering, "tuned for Additive overdubbing so that you can gradually wear out your loop over time by recording it repeatedly"; minimum = bypassed.
- **Layers** knob: 8 levels of undo/redo as a *continuous control* (CCW removes layers, CW adds them back); recording from an earlier layer clears later ones; layers contain all previous audio (no isolating a single layer). **Repeats**: fade per pass, only while recording. **Ramping** of any knob via DIP switches; MIDI for everything.
- Knob movements are recorded too ("if you hear it, so does blooper").

**Chase Bliss Mood MkII (micro-looper + wet channel).** Two channels sharing one **Clock** = sample rate: lower clock = longer, lower-fidelity, darker; the clock steps "in harmonised steps", so a loop *and its reverb* drop an octave together ([product](https://www.chasebliss.com/mood); [manual](https://www.chasebliss.com/s/MOOD-MKII_Manual_Pedal_Chase-Bliss.pdf)). Wet channel: Reverb, Delay, Slip (with Freeze). Micro-looper is **always recording until you engage it**; modes *Env* (playing dynamically interrupts the loop: stutters, stretches, frozen notes), *Tape* (length + speed/direction), *Stretch* (slice size + stretch amount, forward or reverse); endless overdubs; MkII preserves loops across mode changes; routing decides which channel feeds which ([Synth Anatomy](https://synthanatomy.com/2024/03/chase-bliss-mood-mkii-micro-looper-multi-fx-pedal-takes-the-stereo-route.html); [fact sheet](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/622176a9b8d15d57ffbf5700/t/64111e44b7e4db6a404ad61f/1678843461129/MOOD+MKII_Whats+New_Pedal_Chase+Bliss.pdf)). Lesson: a short, always-listening loop that *reacts to your playing envelope* is an instrument in itself.

**Chase Bliss Habit ("echo collector").** Always records a 2–3 minute memory; **Scan** plays fragments of the past – automatically or with the knob from "present" back into the past; **Collect** turns the memory into an ever-accumulating overdub; **Spread** plays two moments of memory at once; modifiers (dissolving echoes, shrinking subdivisions) ([Chase Bliss](https://chasebliss.com/habit); [Guitar.com](https://guitar.com/review/effects-pedal/the-big-review-chase-bliss-habit-echo-collector/)). Lesson: retrospective looping inside a delay – "the past as an instrument".

**Strymon Volante.** Tape/drum/studio echo with a mono **Sound on Sound** looper: reverse (whole loop backwards, keep layering), **Pause** = tape stop/start with mechanical slowdown and speedup, splice, infinite repeat (disengages the record head so the loop stops degrading); half/normal/double speed, lower speed = warmer, more artefacts ([Kraft](https://kraftmusic.com/products/strymon-volante-magnetic-echo-machine-pedal); [Sonicstate](https://sonicstate.com/amped/2019/01/15/magnetic-echo-emulated-in-a-stompbox/)). Lesson: the record head as a switch between "degrading" and "frozen".

**Red Panda Tensor / Particle.** Tensor: tape speed from reverse through stop to forward, **time-stretch up to 4:1 without pitch change**, pitch ±2 oct in fixed intervals, forward/alternate/reverse, **always listening, so you can instantly rewind what you just played**, Hold = loop/overdub/mangle ~4.8–9.4 s, multi-layer randomisation ([zZounds](https://zzounds.com/item--REDTENSOR); [Vintage King](https://vintageking.com/red-panda-tensor)). Particle 2: granular delay/pitch with delay modes random, density, LFO, random pitch, reverse (randomly reversed slices), freeze with threshold ([Premier Guitar review PDF](https://media.rockboard.de/Artikell_PDF/Reviews_PDF/Red%20Panda/10-2019_PremierGuitar_USA_RedPanda_Particle2.pdf); [zZounds](https://www.zzounds.com/item--REDPARTICLE2)).

**Montreal Assembly Count to 5.** Mode 1 pitch-shifting buffer (delay/shimmer); mode 2 a 4 s hold-to-record sampler playing slices with speed, direction, slice size and start randomness ("glitch"); mode 3 an 8 s looper with **three playheads**, each with its own speed and direction – chords and textures from one note; a **Q switch quantises pitch** (chromatic, whole tone, 5ths+octaves) ([Perfect Circuit](https://www.perfectcircuit.com/montreal-assembly-count-to-5-pc-lte-2025.html); [Reverb](https://reverb.com/news/7-pedals-that-actually-do-something-new)). Lesson: multiple heads on one buffer at musically quantised speeds.

**Hologram Microcosm.** Eleven effects in four families – Micro Loop (Mosaic: overlapping loops at different speeds; Sequence: re-orders loop samples into rhythmic sequences; Glide: short loops gliding in pitch), Granules (Haze …), Glitch (Interrupt: pitch-shifted bursts and micro-montages breaking into the dry signal …), Multidelay (Pattern, Warp) – plus a **60 s stereo phrase looper that layers phrases from different effects**, reverse at any point, hold/freeze sampler, tap tempo with subdivisions, MIDI clock ([Guitar World](https://guitarworld.com/reviews/hologram-electronics-microcosm-review); [Synth Anatomy](https://synthanatomy.com/2020/02/microcosm-new-super-creative-granular-looper-pedal-from-hologram.html); [manual](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0920/2928/8752/files/MC_manual_WEB.pdf)). Lesson: the phrase looper *after* a micro-loop/granular engine, so each layer can have a different texture.

**Hologram Infinite Jets.** Two independent samplers re-synthesise each note/chord: Blur (freeze and reshape), Synth, Glitch (real-time re-arranging), Swell (auto swells); triggered by note attacks (mono/poly) or manually; any note can be held indefinitely with a wave shape and decay ([Hologram](https://hologramelectronics.com/infinite-jets); [Pedal of the Day](https://pedal-of-the-day.com/?p=9223)). Lesson: onset-triggered capture – the instrument decides when to sample.

### 1.4 Tape reels, splices, grains

**Make Noise Morphagene.** Recording on the fly, layering with **S.O.S.** (sound-on-sound: the balance between live input and the reel – Sound on Sound found the single knob confusing for telling input from playback), cutting with **Splice**, re-ordering with **Organize**; **Gene-Size** shrinks the splice's playback window into grains, **Slide** moves through them (random access), **Morph** staggers/layers successive genes (pitch-randomised and panned at extremes), **Vari-Speed** sets speed *and direction* with one control; reels up to 2.9 min with ~300 splices stored as WAV; everything voltage-controllable ([manual](https://imagescdn.juno.co.uk/manual/638949-01U.pdf); [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/make-noise-morphagene); [Clockface](https://clockfacemodular.com/en/products/make-noise-morphagene)). Lesson: splices turn a loop into an addressable set of phrases; Organize = "variations of a loop by modulating which splice plays".

**Mutable Instruments Clouds / Beads (manuals read in full).** Clouds "is the exact opposite of a sampler: by default, the module always samples the audio it receives, except when it is in the frozen state"; when frozen, feedback continues in an extra delay/all-pass space, giving a reverb-like build-up ([Clouds manual](https://pichenettes.github.io/mutable-instruments-documentation/modules/clouds/manual/)). Beads ([manual](https://pichenettes.github.io/mutable-instruments-documentation/modules/beads/manual/)):
- "imagine a tape loop, on which incoming audio is continuously recorded … up to 30 replay heads flying along the tape … and there's a reverb".
- Three recording qualities with character: *Cold digital* (Clouds-like), *Sunny tape* (clean 48 kHz), *Scorched cassette* (wow and flutter); feedback limiting matches the medium "from clean brickwall-limiting to grungy tape saturation".
- **Freeze** stops recording; held >10 s the buffer is saved and restored at power-on.
- Grain parameters (TIME = how far back, PITCH ±24 st with notches, SIZE incl. reverse grains and ∞, SHAPE envelope) are **sampled once per grain** – turning PITCH leaves a trail of grains at different pitches instead of shifting all at once.
- Grains latched/clocked/gated; clocked density becomes a divider *or a probability*; **attenurandomisers** (CW = CV amount, CCW = randomisation; internal "peaky" or uniform random).
- Size ∞ = delay or **beat slicer**: with Freeze, a tempo-synced slice of the buffer loops, TIME chooses the slice; tap or clock sets the base time, DENSITY picks binary or wider subdivisions.
- Patch idea: PITCH tracks V/oct – "sequence a melody of grains or even play them from a keyboard"; feed an arpeggio to make each grain a chord tone.

**Ciat-Lonbarde Cocoquantus.** Two independent 8-bit delay/loopers around a five-oscillator analogue "chaos brain"; varispeed so pitch and time bend together; recording time from <1 s to ~1 min depending on speed ([Perfect Circuit explainer](https://www.perfectcircuit.com/signal/ciat-lonbarde-cocoquantus-explained); [Clockface](https://en.clockfacemodular.com/collections/ciat-lonbarde/products/ciat-lonbarde-cocoquantus2)). Lesson: a looper whose clock is itself modulated by an internal living system – close to Myzel's ecological sound sources.

**Teenage Engineering OP-1 field – tape.** Four tracks × ~6 min; tape styles Studio (32-bit, silent), Vintage (wobble, noise), Porta (cassette hiss), Disk Mini; **lift / drop** (cut a take off the tape, re-insert elsewhere – also used to protect a take before overdubbing), a movable loop region, instant variable tape speed incl. while recording, record backwards, "tape tricks" on the number keys (loop points, repeats, reverse, automation); firmware 1.7 (2026) finally added **tape undo (7 levels)** and loop BPM display ([TE tape guide](https://teenage.engineering/guides/op-1/original/tape-mode); [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/reviews/teenage-engineering-op-1-field); [Synth Anatomy](https://synthanatomy.com/2026/04/teenage-engineering-op-1-field-1-7-tape-undo-before-gta-6.html)). Lesson: 15 years without undo shows that tape character alone does not make looping safe; tape *editing* gestures (lift/drop) are a nice alternative to clip thinking.

**Torso Electronics S-4.** Four stereo tracks with a five-stage chain (Material, Granular, Filter, Colour, Space); its Tape material does **varispeed recording, loop overdubbing and time warping**; scenes and eight macro knobs since OS 2.0 ([Rubadub](https://rubadub.co.uk/products/torso-electronics-s4-sculpting-sampler-white); [Moog Audio](https://fr.moogaudio.com/products/torso-electronics-s4-sculpting-sampler-black-1)).

**Teenage Engineering TX-6** is a pocket mixer with built-in effects and a small synth/sequencer, not a looper – not relevant beyond "the mixer as performance surface" *(unverified)*.

### 1.5 Grid, sampler and sequencer instruments

**Elektron Octatrack.** *Pickup machines* = the looper: the first pickup to record becomes the **master** and sets the BPM (manual p. 133 via forum), others take its length ×1, ×2 … (LEN), but pickups cannot be sequenced or p-locked ([Elektronauts](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/how-to-sync-pickup-machines-manually/1422); [Elektronauts](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/pick-up-machine-loop-recording-to-the-songs-bpm/42185)). The richer technique uses **track recorders + Flex machines**: one-shot record trigs (or quantised manual record) sample the input or internal tracks into a buffer while the sequencer runs; several Flex tracks read the same buffer with different slice grids, random slice locks, retrigs – re-sampling something new updates all of them ([Elektronauts live sampling](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/octatrack-live-sampling-tricks/34855); [continuous sampling](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/continuous-live-sampling/9855)). **Scenes + crossfader**: 16 scenes, two assigned to A/B, the crossfader interpolates every locked parameter between them ([OT manual OS 1.25](http://www.elektron.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/octatrack_manual_OS1.25.pdf)). Lesson: the capture buffer and the *sequencer that plays it* are separate; a single fader morphs a whole state.

**Elektron Digitakt / Digitakt II.** Live sampling (400 MB RAM, up to 66 s stereo per sample on DT II) into a sequencer with playback engines Oneshot, Werp, Stretch, Repitch, Grid; real-time record with velocity/retrig modes ([Perfect Circuit](https://www.perfectcircuit.com/elektron-digitakt-ii.html)). Elektron's **live recording of knob moves** writes parameter locks in real time, limited by step resolution (slow moves can miss steps) ([Elektronauts](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/live-record-p-lock-problem/10126); [SOS Model:Cycles](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/elektron-modelcycles?page=2)).

**Polyend Tracker.** Tracker with sampling from mic/line/FM radio; playback modes 1-shot, forward/backward/ping-pong loop, slice, wavetable, granular; **Perform mode** applies global FX moves to individual tracks DJ-style ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/reviews/polyend-tracker); [Synthtopia](https://www.synthtopia.com/content/2020/03/18/polyend-tracker-review-hands-on-demo/)).

**Synthstrom Deluge.** Overdubbing audio looper on the inputs *and* looping MIDI/synth clips with automatic tempo setting; audio clips **record "margins" before and after the loop (even from before you pressed record) to crossfade the seam and avoid clicks**; community firmware added overdub by *resampling the clip's own output*: LOOP extends the sample as you play, LAYER adds layers without extending ([Perfect Circuit](https://perfectcircuit.com/synthstrom-deluge.html); [DelugeFirmware PR #2421](https://github.com/SynthstromAudible/DelugeFirmware/pull/2421)).

**1010music Blackbox / Lemondrop.** Blackbox: live sampling 24/48, MIDI-controlled live looping (Blackbox 2), tempo-synced/trigger/chromatic playback, time-stretch, a launch grid for loops of different lengths ([Synthtopia review](https://www.synthtopia.com/content/2019/05/19/1010music-blackbox-in-depth-review/); [Loopop](https://loopopmusic.com/?p=685)). Lemondrop: four-voice granular synth with two granulators ([Synth Anatomy](https://synthanatomy.com/?p=62775)); live-input capture not confirmed.

**Torso Electronics T-1.** 16-track algorithmic sequencer: Euclidean pulses/steps/rotation, per-step repeats, voicing with voice leading, randomisation of any parameter at any amount, *random sequences that can be looped and scaled*, melody phrases modulating pitch, scale lock ([Synthtopia](https://www.synthtopia.com/content/2022/12/11/torso-electronics-t-1-algorithmic-sequencer-gets-major-update/); [Thomann](https://thomann.co.uk/torso_electronics_t_1.htm)). Lesson: a vocabulary for *mutating symbolic loops* without editing notes.

**Korg Kaossilator 2S** (the closest commercial relative of Myzel's scale field). Touchpad synth: X = pitch locked to a scale/key ("no wrong notes"), Y = tone; a **loop recorder that overdubs touch phrases with undo/redo**; gate arpeggiator; export as multitrack (each overdub a separate track), stereo mix or Ableton set ([Korg](https://www.korg.com/us/products/dj/kaossilator2s)). Lesson: touch-scale instruments and loop overdubbing belong together; per-layer export matters.

**monome norns (softcut) and its loopers.** softcut: 6 voices (play/record heads) on two ~5:49 mono buffers; per voice rate, level, pan with **slew**, **fade_time** for crossfades when cutting/looping, **rec level and pre level** ("full overdub would have both levels set to 1.0 … an echo effect … middle ranges"), pre- and post-record state-variable filters, a routing matrix in which voices can record each other ([softcut studies](https://github.com/monome/docs/blob/gh-pages/norns/softcut.md)). Scripts:
- **mlr** (tehn): six tracks; each grid row is a loop, a key press **cuts the playhead to 1/16 of the loop**, holding two keys makes a sub-loop; speed and reverse per track; cut/stop/start/loop/speed/reverse events can be **quantised**; **pattern recorders** record *gestures* (key presses) as loops with a speed factor, **recalls** store and replay sets of events; 10 ms fade, 60 ms lag between mute and playback ([mlr.lua](https://github.com/tehn/mlr)).
- **oooooo** (schollz): "digital tape loops × 6 – each loop can be leveled, panned, slowed, sped up, shortened, lengthened, overdubbed, destroyed, warbled"; *primed* recording waits for a threshold; per-loop **crossfade** parameter "to control how many transients are present at the beginning of the loop"; random LFOs per loop; **loop destruction** that slowly degrades loops; "chord layering" plays one note at a time from four chords into successive loops ([README](https://github.com/schollz/oooooo)).
- **cheat codes 2** (dndrks): "a sample playground" – three banks of 16 pads, each pad a window on live or loaded buffers, pattern recorders, arps, Euclid, macros, gestural "zilchmos" ([repo](https://github.com/dndrks/cheat_codes_2)) *(pad/buffer details partly unverified)*.
- **reels**: a reel-to-reel metaphor – per track play/pause, record, speed and overdub level on encoders ([repo](https://github.com/itsyourbedtime/reels)).

**Sensory Percussion (Sunhouse).** Sensors learn *where and how* a drum is struck; zones can trigger samples, effects or loops ([Reverb](https://api.reverb.com/news/sensory-percussion-by-sunhouse-more-than-drum-triggers)); no dedicated looper found. Lesson: timbre-zone triggering as control.

### 1.6 Collaborative looping

**Endlesss (Tim Exile, 2019–2024).** A "Jamm" was a shared room with chat; players exchanged "Rifffs" – short multi-layer loops; **every change to a layer created a new Rifff**, so the whole history was kept and could be revisited; built-in instruments, external input, Link sync; it **shut down on 31 May 2024** and users had to export stems before the servers closed ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/gear/tim-exile-endlesss-app-shut-down); [CDM](https://cdm.link/2024/05/endlesss-discontinued/); [Dummy](https://www.dummymag.com/news/collaborative-music-making-app-endless/)). Lessons: (1) an immutable history of loop states is a wonderful undo and a compositional record; (2) cloud dependence kills instruments – Myzel's offline single file is a strength.

## 2. Looping practice

### 2.1 Historical roots

- **Terry Riley – Time-Lag Accumulator (early 1960s).** Two tape recorders with tape running from one to the other; the delay and feedback accumulate semi-improvised saxophone or organ lines into "progressively denser kaleidoscopic textures" (*Poppy Nogood and the Phantom Band*, 1967/68; *A Rainbow in Curved Air*) ([IRCAM](https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/composer/terry-riley/workcourse); [Looper's Delight](https://loopersdelight.com/LDarchive/200410/msg00608.html)). Gesture: *accumulation* – the performer improvises against their own past, which never repeats exactly because feedback < 1.
- **Steve Reich – phasing (1965–67).** *It's Gonna Rain*: the same speech fragment on two machines "lined up in unison, and one of them gradually started getting ahead of the other"; *Come Out*: two channels drift, then four, then eight, until the words dissolve into rhythm and resonance. Reich: a "unison canon" whose rhythmic interval keeps changing ([Wikipedia Come Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Come_Out_(Reich)); [Reich interview](https://stevereich.com/jonathan-cott/)). Gesture: *controlled drift between identical loops*.
- **Pauline Oliveros – Expanded Instrument System.** Grew from the delay between record and playback heads of chained tape machines (1960s, "Tape Delay Techniques for Electronic Music Composition", 1969), later Lexicon delays, then Max software: more than 40 delay times, with chance algorithms (50/50) for delay times, modulation of delayed signals, switching and spatialisation (ambisonic panners); "a device to permit present, past and future to be experienced"; she played it with her accordion ([EIS paper](https://www.academia.edu/25858405/The_Expanded_Instrument_System_Recent_Developments); [Tape Op interview](https://new.tapeop.com/interviews/41/pauline-oliveros); [Cycling '74](https://cycling74.com/2013/12/18/mini-interview-pauline-oliveros)). Gesture: *the delay network as a co-improviser*, many taps, slowly changing, moving in space.
- **Fripp & Eno – Frippertronics (1972 →).** Two Revox machines side by side, tape from the first to the second, the second's output back into the first: a delay of a few seconds set by the distance, repeats governed by the playback level; adapted from Riley's TLA; *(No Pussyfooting)* 1973; Fripp later moved to digital delays ([Wikipedia](https://Wikipedia.com/wiki/Frippertronics); [Guitar Player](https://www.guitarplayer.com/guitarists/robert-fripp-on-the-device-that-made-him-feel-truly-liberated); [Looper's Delight](https://loopersdelight.com/tools/frippertronics/frippertronics.html)). Gestures: *decay as form* (layers fade out unless renewed), long sustained notes and short motifs placed into the moving loop, no fixed downbeat.
- **Brian Eno – Discreet Music (1975) and Music for Airports (1978).** *Discreet Music*: a self-running system of an EMS Synthi with sequencer, a graphic equaliser and two Revoxes with a long delay loop – set up and left to play ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discreet_Music); [Reverb Machine](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/deconstructing-brian-enos-discreet-music)). *Music for Airports*: tape loops of different lengths, "one of the tape loops was seventy-nine feet long and the other eighty-three feet"; in "2/1" seven loops, each a single sung note, of different lengths, so chords form and dissolve "without any discernible structure"; unlike Reich, many *different* phrases on much longer loops ([Reverb Machine](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/deconstructing-brian-eno-music-for-airports/); [Wikipedia Tape loop](https://en.Wikipedia.com/wiki/Tape_loop)). Gesture: *incommensurable loop lengths* = generative harmony.
- **William Basinski – The Disintegration Loops (2001).** 1980s tape loops digitised in 2001; the oxide flaked off at every pass, so the music erased itself in real time over an hour (*dlp 1.1*: 63 min of a short loop decaying) ([SOS Classic Tracks](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/classic-tracks-william-basinski-disintegration-loops?amp); [Futility Closet](https://futilitycloset.com/2017/04/13/the-disintegration-loops)). Gesture: *wear as narrative* – a loop that changes only by losing.

### 2.3 The vocabulary of gestures and structures

| Gesture / structure | What it is | Who / which device |
|---|---|---|
| **Layering (build-up)** | Stack parts one by one; most common form; risk: one long crescendo | Bird, Sultana, Sheeran, Rebillet; every looper |
| **Subtraction / drops** | Mute layers to create sections, breakdowns, the "drop" | Rebillet, Watts; RC mute/stop modes, Loopy mute groups |
| **Decay** | Old material fades unless renewed (feedback < 1) | Frippertronics, Riley; SooperLooper Feedback, Ditto Decay, Blooper Repeats, softcut pre level |
| **Wear / degradation** | Each pass changes the timbre (HF loss, wow, noise, dropouts) | Basinski, Hainbach; Blooper Stability (Additive), oooooo "destruction", Beads "scorched cassette" |
| **Mutation** | The loop changes without being re-played: drop-outs, scrambling, swapping by envelope, random slice locks, probability | Blooper Dropper/Scrambler/Swapper, Morphagene Organize, Octatrack random slices, T-1 |
| **Multiple loop lengths** | Polymeter by integer multiples (×3, ×6) or incommensurable lengths (generative) | Eno, Hainbach; Infinity Sync Multi, oooooo, Loopy |
| **Phasing** | Two identical loops drifting apart | Reich; any looper with fine speed or length nudge |
| **Reversal** | Whole loop backwards, often overdubbed so forward and reverse layers coexist | Volante, DL4, SooperLooper (even during overdub), Blooper |
| **Half speed / octave** | ½ or 2× speed, darker/denser; recording at one speed and playing at another | DL4, Ditto, H90 rec/play speed, OP-1 |
| **Re-pitching in key** | Speed in octaves/fifths (audio) or scale-aware transposition (notes) | Blooper Stepped Speed, Count to 5 Q switch, Beads pitch notches |
| **Trimming / loop within loop** | Shorten from start or end, find a new loop inside | Blooper Trimmer, mlr sub-loops, OP-1 loop region, Morphagene splices |
| **Stutter / repeat** | Repeat the current moment, beat-repeat | Blooper Stutter, Mood Env, RC FX, GarageBand Repeater |
| **Tape stop / start** | Mechanical slow-down as a cadence | Volante Pause, Ditto, Blooper Stopper, GarageBand Remix FX |
| **Freeze / drone bed** | Stop recording, hold the texture, play over it | Clouds/Beads Freeze, Mood, Particle, Myzel "Schlafen" |
| **Granular smear** | Read the loop with many short heads | Beads, Clouds, Morphagene, Microcosm, Mood Stretch |
| **Live resampling / bounce** | Record the output (with effects and space) into a new loop, free tracks, change tempo | Beardyman, RC Bounce, Deluge resample overdub, Octatrack recorders |
| **Imprinting** | Overdub *through* a transformation so it becomes part of the loop | Blooper Additive, SooperLooper rate while recording, DL4 half-time overdub |
| **Scenes / song forms** | Groups of loops switched as sections; series loops of different lengths | RC memories/series, Ditto/Infinity series, Octatrack scenes, Ableton/Bitwig/Loopy follow actions |
| **Call and response** | Loop a phrase, answer it live; with the audience | Rebillet, Watts |
| **Retrospective capture** | Decide after playing what becomes a loop | Ableton Capture, Loopy retrospective, Mood, Habit, Tensor, Myzel Fährte |
| **Gesture looping** | Loop the *control gestures*, not the sound | mlr pattern recorders, Elektron motion recording, Circuit macro recording, Myzel Fährte |

## 3. What makes a looper feel solid vs unreliable

### 3.1 Latency compensation
- Two different latencies matter. SooperLooper: *input latency* decides **what content gets into the loop** ("too low … you can actually get audio that was played before you started the operation … too high you will cut off audio you intended"); *output latency* decides **whether overdubs line up** with what you heard ("too low, overdubs will sound like they are playing too late"); compensation should be off when you monitor through the software, because then you already play to the latency ([doc_sync](https://github.com/essej/sooperlooper/blob/master/doc/html/doc_sync.html), quoting Mobius' calibration guide).
- **Browser:** `AudioContext.outputLatency` (Chrome ≥ 102) estimates the output side and is meant exactly for aligning newly recorded material; nothing equivalent exists for getUserMedia input, so round-trip must be measured (acoustic loopback, echo cancellation off) ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/audio-output-latency); [W3C talk](https://www.w3.org/2021/03/media-production-workshop/talks/slides/hongchan-choi-building-audio-apps.pdf); [public-audio list](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-audio/2014JulSep/0178.html)).
- **Phones:** Google measured an average tap-to-tone latency of 39 ms (28–56 ms) across popular Android phones in 2021 ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2021/03/an-update-on-androids-audio-latency.html)); Bluetooth (SBC/AAC) adds well over 100 ms, often 200 ms ([RTINGS](https://www.rtings.com/headphones/learn/latency); [Outer Audio](https://www.outeraudio.com/?p=59017)) – overdubbing over Bluetooth is effectively impossible.
- **Key point for an instrument that makes its own sound:** when the looper records the instrument's *own rendered output* (or the events at their *render time*), the loop is aligned by construction – the player already plays "into" the instrument's total latency, as when playing live. Compensation is only needed for microphone/line input and for events stamped at touch time. Double compensation is a classic bug.

### 3.2 Loop seams and clicks
- Crossfade at the loop point and at every operation boundary, adjustable (SooperLooper); softcut's `fade_time` for cuts and loops; oooooo exposes the crossfade per loop because it also decides "how many transients are present at the beginning of the loop" ([softcut](https://github.com/monome/docs/blob/gh-pages/norns/softcut.md); [oooooo](https://github.com/schollz/oooooo)).
- A crossfade needs material on both sides: Deluge records **margins** before and after the loop (including audio from before you pressed record) ([Perfect Circuit](https://perfectcircuit.com/synthstrom-deluge.html)); producers "always record the initial loop longer than I need" ([KVR](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=2166642)). Typical crossfades 5–50 ms ([Getly](https://www.getly.store/blog/how-to-loop-audio-seamlessly)).
- **Tails:** a note still ringing when the loop closes must not be cut. Loopy Pro's *tail* blends the post-loop material into the loop start every cycle; *intro* captures what came before ([Loopy forum](https://forum.loopypro.com/discussion/comment/1430895/)). Same for stop: fade-out or stop at loop end instead of a hard cut (RC STOP MODE).
- Zero-crossing edits reduce clicks but "cannot make two unrelated musical moments match" ([Getly](https://www.getly.store/blog/how-to-loop-audio-seamlessly)) – musical seams come from tails and margins, not from sample tricks.
- Beads' FAQ shows the other side: clicks also come from envelope shapes (rectangular grain envelopes) – any transformation (drop, scramble, stutter, trim) needs its own micro-fades ([Beads manual](https://pichenettes.github.io/mutable-instruments-documentation/modules/beads/manual/)).

### 3.3 Quantised vs free start/stop
- Quantised operations wait for the next boundary (cycle, beat, loop) – *show that they are waiting* and let Undo cancel a pending one (SooperLooper).
- **Round mode**: does an operation end where you pressed, or continue to the end of the cycle? (SooperLooper RoundMode; EDP).
- **Rel sync** (start anywhere, length rounded to a multiple of the grid) is the best of both: free gesture, locked result. Loopy's "phase preservation" with threshold and auto count-out does the same: recording starts at the threshold and completes at full loop length aligned to the original timing ([Loopy forum](https://forum.loopypro.com/discussion/comment/749039/)).
- Overdub is never quantised in SooperLooper – you punch in and out where you play.

### 3.4 The first-loop tempo problem
Four strategies, each with costs:
1. **Fixed length + count-in** (RC with rhythm, Loopy count-in/count-out, OP-1 metronome): foolproof, but you must decide length and tempo before playing.
2. **Free first loop sets the tempo** (Ableton Looper Set & Follow, Octatrack pickup master, Headrush auto BPM, Capture MIDI on an empty set): natural for players, but needs a guess of bars/beats; tempo detection jumps by octaves on slow or very fast material (55 vs 110 BPM in Loopy) ([Loopy forum](https://forum.loopypro.com/discussion/comment/749039/); [Ableton help](https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000776450-Capture-MIDI)).
3. **Free loops forever, no tempo** (Frippertronics, Eno, Hainbach): beautiful for ambient, but nothing else can sync.
4. **Retrospective capture** (Capture MIDI, Loopy retrospective, Mood, Fährte): play first, choose later; with a running clock the last N bars are cut on the grid; without one, the system must infer a loop from the played material.
Good designs offer (2)+(4) by default and make the inferred tempo **correctable in one gesture** (×2, ÷2, ±1 bar).

### 3.5 Count-in and threshold start
Count-in for fixed-length recording; threshold ("primed") recording that starts when sound arrives (SooperLooper threshold, oooooo primed, RC AUTO REC, Loopy threshold). Loopy's manual notes they interfere: intro recording needs a count-in and threshold recording off ([Loopy Pro manual](https://loopypro.com/manual/), as quoted in search results).

### 3.6 Overdub variants
Overdub (with feedback), **Replace** (silent while recording), **Substitute** (replace but hear the old), **Multiply** (lengthen by repeating, MultiIncrease), **Insert** (add new cycles), **reverse during overdub**, special endings (end a record directly into overdub/multiply) – SooperLooper's list is the canon. RC DUB MODE overdub/replace; Blooper Additive imprinting; Swapper = envelope-driven replace.

### 3.7 Undo/redo depth
| Device | Undo |
|---|---|
| SooperLooper | "nearly unlimited", oldest states reclaimed when memory runs out; Undo cancels pending ops; long-press = undo all |
| Blooper | 8 layers, as a *knob*; recording from an earlier layer clears later ones |
| RC-505mkII/600 | undo/redo + Mark Back (jump to a marked state) |
| OP-1 field | none for 15 years, 7 levels since fw 1.7 (2026) |
| Kaossilator 2S | undo/redo on the loop recorder |
| Endlesss | every change kept as a new Rifff (full history) |
| Loopy Pro | overdub "peel" (layer-wise removal) *(details unverified)* |
Undo is also performance: the verse/chorus trick with Undo/Redo (SooperLooper), "an obvious mistake repeating infinitely is the last thing you want" ([Reverb tips](https://reverb.com/news/5-essential-live-looping-tips)).

### 3.8 Sync and drift
- MIDI clock jitters; SooperLooper's **play sync** retriggers near boundaries rather than stretching ([doc_sync](https://github.com/essej/sooperlooper/blob/master/doc/html/doc_sync.html)); Loopy's **phase lock** is lost when you change a clip's speed ([Loopy manual via search](https://loopypro.com/manual/)); H90 users reported drift with tempo sync on older firmware ([Eventide forum](https://www.eventideaudio.com/forums/topic/h90-looper-playback-speed-varies-jumps/)).
- Internal drift: a loop length in samples that is not an exact multiple of the clock's bar length accumulates error; loop length must be derived from the clock (or the clock from the loop), never measured twice.
- Tempo change after recording: stretch (quality cost), resample (pitch changes), or retrigger. RC handles ±20 BPM well ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/reviews/boss-rc-505-mkii)).

### 3.9 Visual feedback
Loopy's circular "spinning platter" per loop – tap to record, tap to loop, tap to mute; twist to align the beat; drag one loop onto another to merge ([Michael Tyson on Looper's Delight](https://loopersdelight.com/LDarchive/201102/msg01008.html); [Synthyfrog](https://synthyfrog.com/app/loopy-hd/)). Sheeran's screens mirror the loop state at his feet. Collier adds a visible double for each loop. Myzel's own Fährte concept already says: it must always be visible what is live and what is loop, "sonst wirkt der Klang verhext" (`dev/gestik/konzept.md` §4.4).

### 3.10 Hands-free and one-button control
One button cycles record → overdub/play (Ableton Looper, RC REC ACTION, Blooper left switch); long-press = momentary (SooperLooper SUS); double-tap = undo/clear conventions; foot switches or MIDI (Sheeran FC-300, RC CTL assignments with press/hold/double-click, [Boss](https://in.boss.info/products/rc-505mk2/)). Hands-free alternatives: threshold start + auto count-out (Loopy, RC AUTO REC), record chaining (Loopy HD, [Synthyfrog](https://synthyfrog.com/app/loopy-hd/)), gyro (GarageBand Remix FX), gloves (Heap).

### 3.11 What goes wrong for beginners
- Timing of the first loop (late downbeat) – "the most frequently mentioned challenge" ([Reverb](https://reverb.com/news/5-essential-live-looping-tips); [Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/state-of-the-stomp-looping-tips-and-techniques)).
- No exit strategy: stopping everything to re-record kills the performance; better drop into another loop or mute the bad one.
- Loops louder than the live sound: you then play harder on every overdub – level creep and clipping ([Reverb](https://reverb.com/news/5-essential-live-looping-tips)).
- Too many layers too fast; one endless crescendo; no sections.
- Mode confusion (is it recording, waiting, overdubbing?) and button-combination fiddliness (Morphagene owners: "controls can be a bit fiddly", [Thomann reviews](https://www.thomann.fr/make_noise_morphagene_reviews.htm)).
- Destructive operations without undo (OP-1 before 1.7).

## 4. Symbolic (note/MIDI) vs audio vs parameter looping

| | Audio loop | Event (note/MIDI) loop | Parameter/gesture loop |
|---|---|---|---|
| Captures | The exact sound incl. effects, space, room, randomness of the engine | What was played: pitch, velocity, timing, (MPE/touch) | Control curves: knobs, gyro, faders, XY |
| Can change afterwards | Speed, direction, pitch (interval or stretch), slicing, granular, FX, wear | Instrument, transposition (also *diatonic* in a scale), voicing, quantise/groove, density, probability, re-routing to another sound or external MIDI | Depth, target, scaling, decay, quantisation |
| Cost | Memory (48 kHz stereo float ≈ 0.38 MB/s); DSP small | Tiny memory; *full engine cost at every replay* | Tiny |
| Sound fidelity | Perfect replay of one take; transformations add artefacts (stretch) | Re-rendered: a living, possibly different sound each pass (chaotic or physical models) | — |
| Seams | Crossfades and tails needed | Notes across the boundary: note-off handling, no stuck notes, sustain tails continue naturally | Value jumps at the seam need smoothing |
| Typical problems | Clicks, level creep, stretching artefacts, latency for mic input | Missed/stuck notes at loop wrap (an old live-looping complaint, [Ableton forum](https://forum.ableton.com/viewtopic.php?p=326570)); "lacks the feel" only if quantised or low resolution | Step-resolution loss (Elektron live record misses slow moves) |
Sources for the general trade-off: [Gearspace](https://gearspace.com/threads/midi-vs-audio.698234/), [Betamonkey](https://www.betamonkey.com/midi-drum-loops-or-live-audio-drum-loops/).

**Hybrid designs that exist:**
- *Audio from events*: Ableton Capture MIDI → clip → freeze/bounce to audio; Deluge synth clips + audio clips + resampling; Octatrack recording internal tracks into Flex buffers; RC/Loopy bounce.
- *Events controlling audio*: Beads PITCH tracking V/oct ("play grains from a keyboard"); Blooper sampler mode playing loops at pitches *(MIDI note playback unverified)*; Prismizer/Collier harmoniser (live voice, keyboard-chosen pitches).
- *Gestures looped over sound*: mlr pattern recorders, Elektron motion recording, Circuit macros (6 events per step, [Sonicstate](https://www.sonicstate.com/news/2015/12/04/sonic-lab-novation-circuit-a-new-kind-of-groovebox/)), Myzel Fährte.
- *Per-layer export*: Kaossilator multitrack export, Endlesss stems, Sheeran's FOH stems.

**Assessment.** Audio loops capture what a phrase *was* (space, effect tails, the one-off behaviour of a physical model); event loops capture what it *means* (and stay playable). Parameter loops capture *how it moved*. A strong design records all three together and lets the musician choose per loop – and convert later.

## 5. Phone/touch looping apps and their interaction patterns

- **Loopy HD / Loopy Pro** – the reference for touch looping: circular loop clips with a progress ring; tap to record, tap to close and loop, tap to mute; centre-tap for overdub (Tyson worried it clashed with mute); twist a clip to align the beat; drag a clip onto another to merge, two-finger drag to duplicate; count-in and **record chaining** for hands-free use; a configurable canvas, actions bound to gestures and MIDI, follow actions, Bluetooth/MIDI pedals ([Tyson](https://loopersdelight.com/LDarchive/201102/msg01008.html); [Loopy forum](https://forum.loopypro.com/discussion/comment/1304088/); [manual](https://loopypro.com/manual/)). Tyson's goal: approachable for beginners, power via gestures "easy to perform one-handed while playing an instrument".
- **GarageBand Live Loops (iOS)** – a grid: rows = instruments (one cell per row plays), columns = scenes triggered together; tap or double-tap a cell to record into it with the row's Touch Instrument; **Time Snap** quantises starts/stops ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/garageband-iphone/chsd95b06794/ios)). **Remix FX**: Reverse, Scratch, Tape Stop buttons; Gater, Downsampler sliders; XY pads with Filter, Wobble, Orbit, Repeater, Reverb, Delay; **Gyro Control** of the XY pads and **FX Lock** to keep an effect after lifting the finger ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/garageband-iphone/chsabb64c325/ios); [TapSmart](https://www.tapsmart.com/tips-and-tricks/fun-garageband-no-musical-talent/)).
- **Koala Sampler** – mic-first: record into pads, make beats, resample the output through the effects back into a pad; "instant music progress … rather than burying you in parameters"; a reviewer missed non-destructive layering (layering means resampling) ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/koala-sampler-beat-maker/id1449584007)).
- **BeatMaker 3** – Scene Mode for looping and arranging clips live, audio tracks with Elastique stretch/pitch, Link ([Synth Anatomy](https://synthanatomy.com/?p=12407)).
- **Blocs Wave (Novation/Ampify)** – 48 loop pads with real-time stretching, mic recording, Ableton export, Link ([Novation](https://novationmusic.com/software/blocs-wave-ios)); **Groovebox** – pattern instruments, MIDI in, Ableton export ([Ask.Audio](https://ask.audio/articles/review-ampify-groovebox)).
- **Ableton Note** – phone sketchpad with Live-style **Capture**: jam on pads, the app remembers the last round; 8 tracks × 8 clips × 8 scenes, double loops, duplicate clips ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/ableton-note-app-iphone-ableton-live); [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/ableton-note)).
- **Endlesss** – one-screen jam with up to a handful of layers per Rifff, every change saved, shared in real time (closed 2024).

**Patterns that recur:** big targets per loop (circle or cell) with a progress indication; tap-cycle state machines; long-press and double-tap as secondary actions; drag between loops for merge/copy; grids of scenes; quantised launch with visual waiting state; XY performance effects with lock and gyro; capture-after-the-fact; one-handed operation; counting-in so the other hand can play; export of stems.

## 6. Ideas worth taking for Myzel (ranked)

Context assumed from the repo: Myzel already has an always-running Puls clock (also when the Puls is off), **Fährte** (retrospective, bar-based, decaying loops of Gestik axes and any continuous control – `dev/gestik/konzept.md` §4.4), Szenen, **Schlafen** (flip the phone = freeze cadence), a mixer, MIDI in/clock, mic access, a sample library and 28 engines, many of them physical or chaotic (the same notes never sound exactly the same twice). The looper should be the musical sibling of Fährte, not a separate world.

1. **Every take is captured twice: as events and as audio ("lebendig" and "Tonband").** Record the played events (notes with touch data, Gestik/Fährte curves, preset/engine) *and* the rendered audio of the same pass, sample-aligned. Per loop the player chooses: *lebendig* = re-rendered live by the engine every pass (editable, transposable in the scale field, playable on any of the 28 sources, sounds slightly different every time with Amöbe/Rhizom/Glut – a feature), or *Tonband* = the exact sound with its space and effect tails, mangleable like tape. Switching is lossless both ways ("freeze this living loop", "bring this tape back to life"). No other phone looper offers this for a synth with physical models (closest: Ableton Capture + freeze, Deluge, Octatrack recorders). Cost: audio memory + engine cost while "lebendig" – measured honestly, never a reason to drop one of the two.
2. **Nothing is ever missed – retrospective capture of audio and events.** Extend Fährte's principle: Myzel always keeps the last N bars (e.g. 16 bars or 60 s) of rendered audio and events. One tap = the last 1/2/4/8 bars become a loop on the bar grid; hold = record from the next bar; free mode = infer the phrase length and tempo from the last played phrase, with one-tap ×2/÷2/±1-bar correction (Ableton Capture, Loopy retrospective, Mood, Habit, Tensor). This removes the beginner's downbeat problem entirely.
3. **Seams worthy of the engines.** Record margins on both sides of every loop and every punch (Deluge); fold the ringing tail of the last pass into the loop start (Loopy tail) so a Sehne or Gespinst note that crosses the loop point rings on instead of being cut; equal-power crossfades whose length adapts to the material (short for percussive, longer for drones; oooooo/SooperLooper adjustable); stopping a loop lets its tails ring out (stop at loop end / fade / immediate, RC). For event loops: notes held across the seam continue, no forced retrigger, never a stuck note. Micro-fades on every drop/scramble/stutter (Beads lesson).
4. **Layers as objects, decay as a gain – non-destructive Frippertronics.** Store each overdub as its own layer (delta), not baked in: per-layer mute/solo/level, unlimited undo/redo within the memory budget, "peel" the last layer, recall the verse/chorus via undo/redo (SooperLooper). Decay ("Verblassen", like Fährte's 0.85) can be applied as an age-dependent gain and, for wear, an age-dependent filter/wow/noise – so it is reversible ("revive a faded layer") until the player consolidates. Offer *destructive* Additive imprinting as a deliberate choice (Blooper), and bounce/consolidate to free memory. Mind the overdub sum: headroom and a transparent limiter (SooperLooper's slightly-below-100 % feedback).
5. **Musical time transforms, quantised to the scale.** Varispeed with tape inertia (Augustus, Volante Pause, Blooper Smooth Speed), half/double speed, reverse at the next boundary or immediately, tape stop/start as a cadence; **stepped speed in octaves and fifths** for audio loops (Blooper, Count to 5), and for event loops **transposition by scale degrees inside Myzel's scale field** (impossible for audio, trivial for events – a unique point). Separate record and play speed (H90, DL4 half-speed): record at half speed, play at normal. Use the best resampler (windowed-sinc/polyphase) for speed changes and the best available stretch only where pitch must stay fixed – honest cost.
6. **Many loops, many lengths: polymeter and phasing by design.** Each loop has a length relation: *Takt* (multiple or fraction of the Puls bar, including ×3, ×5, ×6, Infinity-style), *frei* (asynchronous, Eno/Hainbach), or *Phase* (identical copy with a slow drift per cycle, Reich). Show all loops as concentric rings around the scale field so the phase relations are visible (Loopy circles). Free loops do not sync on purpose; Takt loops never drift (length derived from the clock in samples).
7. **Mutation without re-playing (event and audio).** A "Wandel" control per loop: probability drop-outs (Dropper), re-order by beat slices (Scrambler/Organize/Octatrack random slices), stutter last/next beat (Stutter), envelope-ducking so the loop makes room when you play (Swapper/Mood Env), thinning/densifying, rotate, retrograde/inversion within the scale, humanise or tighten (T-1, Circuit). Each mutation previewable, then either kept as a modulation or imprinted.
8. **Form: scenes, series, follow actions, momentary launch.** Loops belong to Szenen; scene changes quantised to bar or loop end with a visible countdown; series loops of different lengths for verse/chorus (Ditto, Infinity); follow actions ("after 4 passes → next scene / mute this / thin out") for song forms and generative pieces (Ableton, Bitwig, Loopy); press-and-hold = momentary loop or scene, release returns (Bitwig release actions) – ideal for touch.
9. **Live resampling and loops that feed the engines.** Bounce any mixer bus (or the master incl. reverb) into a new loop; and – unique to Myzel – route a loop *into* an engine as excitation or input: the string net of Gespinst, Glut's feedback, Sehne's E-Bow driven by a loop (Clouds/Beads feedback, softcut voices recording each other, Oliveros' delay network, Cocoquantus). Loops become a living part of the ecology, not just playback.
10. **A granular/micro-loop reading mode per loop.** Gene size, slide, density, spread, pitch per grain with notches, randomisers, reverse grains, freeze (Morphagene, Beads, Mood Stretch, Microcosm Mosaic/Haze). Ties naturally to Schlafen (freeze) and to Gestik (tilt = position/slide, Bebung = grain jitter).
11. **Hands-free control that fits Gestik's rules.** No new accidental gestures; instead: threshold-armed recording and auto count-out after N bars (Loopy, RC AUTO REC, oooooo primed); MIDI learn for the nanoKEY/any MIDI pedal; **Bluetooth page-turner pedals** (they appear as keyboards – arrow/space keys – which a web page can read); GarageBand-style gyro control *of loop effects* (varispeed, filter, slide) via the existing Gestik axes with an "FX lock" to keep the setting when the phone is put down.
12. **One-button state machine with forgiving secondaries.** Tap empty loop = record (or capture last N bars, user choice); tap while recording = close and play (or overdub, REC ACTION style); tap playing = mute/unmute at the boundary; long-press = momentary/overdub while held (SooperLooper SUS); double-tap = undo last layer; clear only with undo available. Pending actions visibly waiting; Undo cancels them.
13. **Visible truth.** Per loop: ring with position and bar ticks, layer stack, mode (lebendig/Tonband), decay state, pending action; live vs loop distinction everywhere (Fährte's ghost spore); optional "metronome-free" free mode with its own visual pulse; input level and overdub headroom.
14. **Keep and share.** Loops saved with scene/preset (as Fährte), export of each loop/layer as WAV and each event loop as a MIDI file (Kaossilator, Endlesss, Sheeran's FOH stems); everything offline (Endlesss' fate).
15. **Mic loops as a first-class citizen, calibrated.** Voice or an acoustic instrument via the mic with measured round-trip compensation (reuse Myzel's tap/clap measurement, separate input/output as in SooperLooper), echo cancellation off, a Bluetooth warning; such loops can then be granulated, fed into engines (idea 9) or harmonised by the scale field (Prismizer-style, a later stage).

## 7. Pitfalls to avoid

- **Clicks and cut tails** at loop points, punch-ins, mutes, stops and every slice transformation. No margins = no good crossfade.
- **Double or missing latency compensation.** Internal capture needs none; mic input needs input *and* output compensation; events must be stamped at render time, not touch time. Bluetooth output makes overdubbing impossible – detect/warn.
- **Timing in the main thread.** Loop boundaries, quantised actions and event playback belong in the audio clock (AudioWorklet / scheduler look-ahead), never in setTimeout or rAF; background tabs and screen lock throttle the main thread (wake lock).
- **Drift.** Loop length must be an exact function of the clock (or vice versa); with external MIDI clock, resync at boundaries (play sync) instead of letting jitter accumulate; phase lock must survive speed changes or say clearly that it doesn't (Loopy).
- **Tempo inference errors** (octave errors on slow material) without a one-tap correction.
- **Level creep and clipping** in overdubs; loops louder than live playing; muddy stacks without per-layer level/filter.
- **Hidden modes.** Waiting, armed, recording, overdubbing, lebendig/Tonband, decaying – each must be visible; Morphagene/Octatrack-style button combinations are fatal on a phone.
- **Destructive operations without undo** (clear, re-record, imprint, consolidate). Fifteen years of OP-1 users show the cost.
- **Memory surprises on phones.** Float 48 kHz stereo ≈ 0.38 MB/s – layers, retrospective buffers and margins add up; budget and display it, consolidate on purpose, never silently downgrade quality (owner's rule: best sound; use Int16 storage only if measured transparent and agreed).
- **Stretching where resampling would do.** Time-stretch artefacts on physical-model tails are audible; prefer pitch-coupled varispeed unless pitch must stay, and then use the best algorithm.
- **Event-loop hazards:** stuck notes at clear/stop/scene change, notes lost at the wrap, double triggers when a held note crosses the seam, engines with long tails retriggered every pass.
- **Gesture conflicts.** Do not add looper gestures that collide with Wenden/Schlafen, Schütteln/Ladung or Fährte; Gestik's "few, robust gestures" rule stands.
- **One endless crescendo.** Without subtraction, scenes and decay, live loops become a single build-up; ship scenes, mute groups and decay from day one.
- **Cloud or account dependence** (Endlesss): keep the looper fully offline in the single file.
